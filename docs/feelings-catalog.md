# Feelings catalog

A short write-up for every word on the wheel (E5 vocabulary,
`src/feelings-wheel.ts`): what the word means here, and how to recognise
it from the inside. It has two jobs:

1. **User guide.** When two chips both feel plausible, the entries and
   their *Boundary* notes give the distinguishing test.
2. **Clarity audit.** Every word carries exactly one meaning in this
   catalogue. If an entry is hard to write without overlapping a
   neighbour, that is a taxonomy bug — file it against the wheel, not the
   catalogue.

Conventions: the wheel records the bare word you pick, so each word is
described as a state you could notice *right now*. Middle words carry a
**Signs** list (body, behaviour, thought markers). Finer words get one or
two sentences each, with the identification cue built in. Words are plain
British register; where a word sits close to one in another sector, a
*Boundary* line names the test that separates them (the cross-sector pairs
are collected in [Deliberate near-pairs](#deliberate-near-pairs) at the
end).

## angry

Something is wrong and the energy points **outward** — at a person, a
situation, an unfairness. Anger has a target and a charge: heat, a raised
voice waiting to happen, an urge to confront, correct, or blame.

**Boundary:** if the sharpness has no real grievance behind it — small,
ordinary things are setting you off because there is nothing left to
absorb them — that is `overloaded > boiling over`, not anger.

### irritated

Low-grade anger at frictions and annoyances. The grievances are small and
usually legitimate — noise, interruptions, slowness, other people's habits
— but the reaction is starting to leak out. It passes when the friction
stops.

**Signs:** sighing, tutting, terse replies; a specific *thing* you could
name that is getting on your nerves; relief when it stops.

- **annoyed** — mild irritation at one nameable thing. The baseline word:
  you know exactly what it is and it is not a big deal, but it registers.
- **impatient** — irritation specifically at waiting or slowness: a queue,
  a spinner, a person taking too long. The tell is time — checking the
  clock, urging things along.
- **grumpy** — a general bad-tempered mood not pinned to one cause;
  everything gets the same slightly sour reception. Often carried in from
  poor sleep or a bad morning.
- **snappy** — the irritation is escaping as short, sharp replies to
  people. Identified in your own mouth: you hear the edge as you speak.
  *Boundary:* if the snapping has no grievance and tracks how depleted you
  are, see `boiling over > tetchy`.
- **huffy** — irritation at feeling slighted or not taken seriously; a
  sulky, offence-taking edge. The tell is that it is about how *you* were
  treated, and part of you wants the other person to notice.
- **prickly** — touchy; approaches, questions, or feedback that would
  normally be fine are landing as criticism. Identified by other people
  suddenly seeming to handle you carefully.

### frustrated

Anger at being blocked. You are trying to get somewhere — a task, a goal,
a conversation — and something keeps stopping you. The energy is still
pointed at finishing; that is what separates it from giving up.

**Signs:** repeated attempts at the same thing; muttering at objects or
software; the urge to force, shove, or shortcut; effort rising as progress
stalls.

- **blocked** — a specific obstacle stands between you and the goal and
  you cannot currently get past it. You know what the wall is.
- **thwarted** — blocked by someone or something that *acted* — a
  decision, a rule, a person — rather than by chance. Carries a "they
  stopped me" flavour.
- **exasperated** — frustration at something that keeps happening after
  you have already dealt with it repeatedly. The tell is the word
  "again" — *not this again*.
- **held up** — delayed rather than defeated: the path is intact but the
  timeline is slipping and it is not in your control.
- **fed up** — the accumulated form: patience for a recurring situation
  has run out. Less spike, more "I have had enough of this."
- **hampered** — able to proceed, but everything is harder than it should
  be; friction rather than a wall. Tools, conditions, or constraints are
  costing you effort on every step.

### resentful

Anger with a ledger. Somebody got, took, or was given something at your
expense — credit, effort, fairness — and the account has not been settled.
Resentment is quieter and longer-lived than hot anger; it replays.

**Signs:** re-running the unfair scene; score-keeping ("after everything I
did"); coolness toward a specific person; the grievance is in the past but
still warm.

- **bitter** — resentment that has soaked in and gone stale; the
  unfairness colours how you talk about the whole topic, not just the
  incident.
- **indignant** — the fresh, righteous sense of having been treated
  unfairly and having a case; you could state the wrong done to you in one
  sentence.
- **jealous** — resentment aimed at someone having what you want — a
  possession, a role, attention. Identified by the comparison: the feeling
  tracks *them* having it.
- **sore** — still smarting about a particular loss or slight and not
  ready to be talked out of it; "sore about it" is exactly the register.
- **used** — you gave effort, help, or trust and it was consumed without
  return or acknowledgement. The tell is feeling like a resource rather
  than a person.
- **cheated** — a bargain was broken: the rules, promise, or deal you
  relied on did not pay out. There is a specific "that's not what was
  agreed" at the centre.

### hostile

High-heat anger. The charge is strong enough that it wants discharge — at
a person, out loud, now. This is the intensity band, whatever the cause;
if the heat is overflow from overload rather than a grievance, see
`overloaded > boiling over`.

**Signs:** heat in the face and chest; clenched jaw or fists; rehearsing
what you will say or do to them; struggle to speak civilly.

- **furious** — full-strength anger at something nameable; controlled on
  the outside or not, inside it is at the top of the dial.
- **seething** — furious *and contained*: outwardly functioning while the
  anger rolls underneath. Identified by the gap between your face and your
  insides.
- **fuming** — visibly not over it: replaying the provocation and radiating
  it — pacing, exhaling hard, unable to drop the subject.
- **raging** — the containment is failing; the anger is coming out in
  voice, body, or action, or would if you let it.
- **vengeful** — the anger has a plan: thoughts have moved from *what they
  did* to *what I will do back*. The rehearsal of payback is the tell.
- **spiteful** — wanting to hurt back in small ways — the cutting remark,
  the withheld favour — even at cost to yourself. Meaner and pettier than
  vengeful, and you know it.

### hurt

Anger tangled with a wound. Someone whose opinion or behaviour matters
to you did the damage, and under the anger there is an ache. Hurt looks
like anger from the outside and like sadness from the inside.

**Signs:** the incident involves a *person who matters*; urge to withdraw
from them as much as to confront; replaying what they said with a sting
each time.

- **betrayed** — trust was broken by someone inside your circle: they knew
  what it would mean and did it anyway. The wound is the broken trust more
  than the act itself.
- **wronged** — a clear injustice was done to you personally and it stands
  uncorrected; you want it *acknowledged* as much as fixed.
- **stung** — a sharp, fast hurt from a remark or act that got through
  your guard; small on paper, but it landed and it smarts.
- **disrespected** — your competence, character, or worth was disparaged,
  directly or by implication, and the disrespect is the injury.
- **offended** — something said or done crossed a line of respect or
  decency that matters to you; the reaction defends the line, not just
  yourself.
- **dismissed** — your input, feeling, or presence was waved away as not
  worth engaging with. Identified by the peculiar sting of not even being
  argued with.

### critical

Anger in the judging register: the heat has cooled into assessment, and
the assessment is negative. Aimed at people, work, institutions — or
yourself. The stance looks down at its object.

**Signs:** running commentary of what is wrong with the thing; eye-rolling
(inner or outer); comparisons in which the object loses; hard to name
anything good about it.

- **scornful** — active contempt: the object is beneath serious
  consideration and part of you enjoys saying so.
- **sceptical** — pre-emptive doubt about claims and motives: assuming
  self-interest, spin, or failure before evidence arrives. The tell is
  that good news gets discounted automatically.
- **disgusted** — a visceral recoil — moral or physical — from something
  that violates a standard; the body pulls back or curls a lip before the
  thought finishes.
- **unimpressed** — the thing was presented as good and does not meet the
  bar. Cool rather than hot; a flat "is that it?"
- **appalled** — shocked *and* condemning: what you have just seen or
  learned is far below any acceptable standard and you can hardly credit
  it.
- **judgmental** — you notice yourself scoring people on things that are
  not really your business, and being ungenerous about it. Self-aware
  word: picking it is half admission.

## fearful

Threat detection is running: something bad could happen, might be
happening, or is coming. Fear points at the **future or the uncertain**,
and it is fed by thoughts — what-ifs, worst cases, vigilance.

**Boundary:** distress that rises and falls with *present sensory input*
(noise, light, crowds) is `overloaded > overstimmed`, not fear — the test
is whether it tracks your thoughts or the environment
(`failure-modes/anxiety-vs-sensory-overload`).

### anxious

Ambient, forward-pointing worry. A possible bad outcome (or several,
unnamed) is occupying the background, and body and mind are held slightly
braced for it. Nothing is wrong *right now* — that is the signature.

**Signs:** what-if thoughts; restless scanning back to the same topic;
stomach or chest involvement; trouble settling into anything absorbing.

- **worried** — anxiety with a named object: a person, result, deadline,
  or bill you keep returning to. You can say what it is about.
- **uneasy** — something feels off without a clear reason; low-level
  disquiet that resists being pinned down.
- **nervous** — anticipatory jitters about a specific upcoming event —
  interview, appointment, conversation. Rises as it approaches; resolves
  when it is over.
- **keyed up** — wound tight with anticipation: alert, over-ready,
  slightly performance-charged. The energy is aimed at the coming thing.
  *Boundary:* if the tension is accumulated pressure rather than
  anticipation of an event, see `boiling over > wound up`.
- **jittery** — the anxiety is in the body as fine agitation: fidgeting,
  restless legs, caffeine-feeling. *Boundary:* if it tracks sensory input
  rather than worry, see `overstimmed > twitchy`.
- **tense** — braced muscles: shoulders, jaw, gut held tight against
  something that has not happened. Found by body-scan more than by
  thought.

### panicked

Acute alarm — the fear system has fired at full strength. Heart, breath,
and thought all accelerate; the range of what you can consider narrows to
escape and safety.

**Signs:** racing heart, fast shallow breath; thought narrowed to one
track; urgent need to move, leave, or fix *now*; time distortion.

- **terrified** — extreme fear of something specific and imminent; the
  full-body version, whether or not you can move.
- **breathless** — the fear is in your breathing: tight chest, can't get a
  full breath, air hunger out of proportion to exertion.
- **alarmed** — the jolt of a threat just registering: something has
  changed and the system has gone to red before you have assessed it.
- **frantic** — panic converted into scattered action: rushing, searching,
  doing three things at once badly, unable to slow down and think.
  *Boundary:* if the discharge is involuntary overflow rather than
  goal-directed rushing, see `boiling over > meltdown`.
- **shaky** — the adrenaline is visible: trembling hands, unsteady voice
  or legs, during or just after the spike.
- **spooked** — startled into wariness by something sudden or eerie; the
  event has passed but the system will not stand down.

### insecure

Fear about your own standing: not good enough, about to be found out, or
unsafe in your position with others. The threat is to how you are seen or
valued.

**Signs:** comparing yourself to others and losing; rehearsing or editing
yourself before speaking; seeking reassurance and not being settled by
it.

- **inadequate** — measured against the task or the company, you come up
  short; the gap between what is needed and what you have feels like
  fact.
- **unsure** — low confidence in your own judgement or choice right now;
  decisions keep reopening themselves.
- **exposed** — visible without cover: your work, feelings, or flaws are
  on display and anyone can take a shot at them.
- **unworthy** — the sense of not deserving what you have or being owed
  your place; praise bounces off it.
- **inferior** — diminished in your own eyes next to someone or something;
  shrunk, junior, negligible.
- **intimidated** — a specific person or setting makes you shrink and
  self-censor; capability drops in their presence.

### dreading

Fear with a date on it. Something specific and unwanted is coming — an
event, a conversation, a result — and its approach colours everything
between now and then.

**Signs:** counting down; the thing sits on tomorrow like weather;
temptation to cancel or postpone; relief fantasies about it being called
off.

- **sinking** — the stomach-drop as the unwanted thing gets close or gets
  real: the calendar reminder, the name on the incoming call.
- **daunted** — the size of what is ahead makes you feel too small for
  it before you have begun.
- **wary** — guarded and watchful around a person, place, or step that
  has bitten you before; proceeding, but with your hand on the rail.
- **hesitant** — you will probably do the thing, but every part of you is
  dragging; consent without willingness.
- **queasy** — the dread is in your stomach: mild nausea, appetite gone,
  gut registering what the mind is minimising.
- **on eggshells** — dread of *setting someone off*: monitoring your own
  words and steps around a volatile person or situation.

### helpless

Fear plus no available move. The threat or situation is real, and nothing
you can do changes it — the controls are outside your reach. Often the
worst-feeling state in this sector because agency itself is gone.

**Signs:** searching for options and finding none, repeatedly; waiting on
other people's decisions; the specific despair of "nothing I do
matters here."

- **trapped** — boxed into a situation — job, obligation, room, dynamic —
  with the exits closed or too costly to take.
- **cornered** — trapped *and under pressure*: someone or something is
  advancing and there is nowhere to give ground.
- **powerless** — the outcome that matters is entirely in other hands;
  your effort has no purchase on it.
- **defenceless** — exposed to harm with no shield: whatever is coming,
  you cannot block it, only take it.
- **desperate** — the situation is bad enough that you are considering
  options you would normally never touch; urgency has outrun judgement.
- **adrift** — unmoored rather than pinned: no direction, no anchor,
  nothing to steer by. The drifting itself is the fear.

### confused

Fear's quieter cousin: the model of what is happening has broken down.
You cannot make the situation add up, and the not-knowing is itself
unsettling.

**Signs:** re-reading, re-asking, re-checking without gaining traction;
inability to summarise the situation; decisions deferred because the
picture will not resolve.

- **perplexed** — thoroughly unable to make sense of what is happening;
  the situation has outrun your model entirely.
- **lost** — without bearings in a task, plan, or life stretch: you do not
  know where you are in it or what the next step is.
- **torn** — pulled between two options or loyalties with real weight on
  both sides; choosing either loses something.
- **muddled** — thoughts tangled: pieces of the problem are all present
  but will not line up in order. *Boundary:* if thinking is slow-and-blank
  from depletion rather than tangled, see `drained > foggy`.
- **blindsided** — something arrived from an angle you were not watching;
  the shock of not having seen it coming is the feeling.
- **flustered** — scrambled by pressure in the moment: dropping threads,
  saying the wrong word, doing steps out of order while others watch.

## overloaded

Demand has exceeded capacity — the body-budget axis. This sector is
**not** a flavour of fear, sadness, or anger: it is what happens when
input, demands, or unprocessed charge outrun what the system can hold
(`failure-modes/hyperarousal-vs-hypoarousal`). Crossing the limit pushes
in one of two directions — escalating and outward (`boiling over`) or
collapsed and inward (`shut down`) — with `overwhelmed` and `overstimmed`
as the flooded approach to the line, `stuck` as attention captured beyond
choice, and `drained` as the depleted aftermath.

**Reach for this sector when** the honest answer to "what's wrong?" is
*nothing in particular — there is just too much*, or when body and
behaviour have changed (snappiness, slowness, silence) without a
grievance, threat, or loss to explain them.

### overwhelmed

More is arriving than can be processed — tasks, emotions, information,
all of it at once. Capacity itself is the problem: the individual pieces
might each be manageable, but the total is not.

**Signs:** cannot pick a starting point; small additional asks feel
enormous; urge to escape the whole situation rather than any one part;
lists and inputs blurring into a single mass.

- **flooded** — inputs or emotions arriving faster than you can process
  them; the hit-all-at-once form. Thought is still happening but is
  under water.
- **swamped** — buried in *demands*: the to-do side has overrun what a day
  or a person can hold. The workload word.
- **maxed out** — at absolute capacity: nothing more fits, and the next
  thing added will displace something or break something.
- **buried** — so far under that you have lost sight of the top; effort
  now goes into digging, not progressing.
- **pressured** — still coping, but with zero slack: the demands press
  from every side, everything is covered thinly, and one wobble will tear
  it. The early-warning word in this set.
- **literal** — nuance has gone offline: you notice yourself reading
  everything at face value, applying rules rigidly, missing tone and
  subtext you would normally catch. A capacity marker — subtlety is
  expensive, and the budget is spent (*Unmasked* ch. 3;
  `failure-modes/stuck-in-outdated-coping`).

### overstimmed

Sensory overload: present input — noise, light, touch, motion, people —
exceeds what can currently be processed. Externally driven by definition:
it tracks the environment, rising with input and easing when the input
stops (`failure-modes/anxiety-vs-sensory-overload`,
`telemetry-state/sensory-over-responsivity`).

**Signs:** distress rises and falls with the sensory environment;
ordinary sounds or lights register as aggressive; strong urge for quiet,
dark, or solitude; relief is fast once the input stops.

- **rattled** — composure knocked by noise, chaos, or a sudden event and
  not yet recovered; shaken loose rather than hurt.
- **jarred** — one sharp input — a noise, an interruption, an abrupt
  change — jolted the system and the jolt is still ringing.
- **frayed** — worn thin by *sustained* input: the insulation is worn
  through and every further stimulus lands directly on the nerve.
- **buzzing** — the overstimulation hum: a physical vibration or static in
  the head or body that continues after the input, like ears ringing
  after a concert but system-wide.
- **twitchy** — skin-level physical agitation: can't sit still, clothes
  and contact register too much, small sounds produce flinches.
  *Boundary:* if the restlessness is fed by worry rather than input, see
  `anxious > jittery`.
- **too much** — the plain community phrase: everything — light, sound,
  people, all of it — is more than can be taken in, and the only clear
  signal left is the need for it to stop.

### boiling over

The hyperarousal direction: unreleased charge — sensory, emotional, or
both — is building toward the upper limit. This is the escalating,
outward-pointing state that generic wheels file under anger, which is
exactly how it gets misread: there is no real grievance, only pressure
(`failure-modes/meltdown`, `failure-modes/hyperarousal-vs-hypoarousal`).
Catching the earlier words in this set is the practical point — after a
certain line, the state runs its course.

**Signs:** reactions arriving disproportionate to their triggers; heat
and agitation without a target that deserves it; feeling one small thing
away from shouting or crying; others starting to handle you carefully.

- **meltdown** — the threshold crossed: charge discharging outward
  involuntarily — crying, shouting, hard stimming, saying things sideways
  — with reasoning offline until it has run its course. Not a tantrum and
  not a choice; often the honest after-the-fact record for a check-in
  that was missed during it.
- **simmering** — the pre-state: heat building steadily under a still
  surface. Contained for now, but you can feel that containment is doing
  work.
- **wound up** — tension accumulated until the body is coiled: unable to
  settle, sit, or let a topic drop. *Boundary:* anticipation of a coming
  event is `anxious > keyed up`; this is accumulated pressure with no
  event needed.
- **on edge** — hair-trigger: braced such that the next small thing — one
  more noise, one more ask — may tip you over. Filed here rather than
  under fear because the brace comes from load, not from a feared future
  (`failure-modes/neurodivergence-misread-as-anxiety`).
- **tetchy** — small, reasonable asks are drawing sharp responses.
  Feels like being angry at everyone; the tell that it is load, not
  anger, is that there is no grievance you could actually state.
- **short fuse** — the gap between trigger and reaction has collapsed:
  things you would normally absorb are going straight to the reaction.
  Noticed most reliably in retrospect over the last hour.

### stuck

Attention captured beyond choice. A task, thought, or urge has hold of
you and will not release — the defining feature is that **stopping is
what's difficult** (`failure-modes/hyperfocus`,
`failure-modes/autistic-inertia`). Pointed at distress it is rumination;
pointed at a task it is hyperfocus; either way it runs the meter — it
consumes capacity, bypasses body signals, and blocks whatever else the
day needed (`capacity-load/rumination-as-continuous-cognitive-demand`).

**Signs:** repeated failed attempts to disengage; meals, breaks, or
bedtime sliding past unnoticed; the same content on re-run without new
information; other tasks orbiting unstarted.

**Boundary:** the difference from `calm > focused` is choice and cost —
focused engagement can be put down when something else matters and
leaves you fed; stuck cannot, and leaves you wrecked or guilty.

- **hyperfocus** — absorbed involuntarily in one task: hours vanish,
  hunger and time go unregistered, and switching away feels physically
  resistant (`capacity-load/hyperfocus-bypasses-interoception-and-time`).
  Productive, possibly — but not chosen and not stoppable.
- **wired** — switched on and revving with no off-ramp: mind racing, body
  activated, rest impossible even when tired underneath ("tired but
  wired").
- **looping** — the same thoughts replaying without new information
  arriving; each pass adds repetition, not progress. The neutral-content
  version of ruminating.
- **ruminating** — dwelling on something distressing or self-critical:
  why-questions and replays that circle without resolving
  (`failure-modes/rumination-spiral`). The test: the facts have not
  changed since the last pass, but the distress has grown.
- **obsessing** — attention pinned to one topic, worry, or person; every
  time you free it, it snaps back there. Content-agnostic — the pinning
  is the feeling.
- **compelled** — urge-driven: feeling *made* to keep working, checking,
  or doing, with the sense of choice absent. Picking this word is the
  observation that the urge is running you, not the other way round.

### shut down

The hypoarousal direction: capacity exceeded and the system has dropped —
a protective collapse, not a decision (`failure-modes/shutdown`). Speech,
thought, feeling, and action go offline in some combination; from outside
it is misread as sulking or unhelpfulness, which is exactly backwards.
The finer words name **which channel went offline**.

**Signs:** responses slow or stop; words hard to produce; strong pull
toward dark, quiet, and lying down; pressure from others deepens it
rather than shifting it.

- **frozen** — the action channel: you cannot initiate movement or the
  next step, even a wanted one. Sitting on the bed unable to start the
  shower is the shape.
- **numb** — the feeling channel: emotions have gone offline and nothing
  registers — not the things that should hurt, not the things that should
  please. Distinct from sad: there is no ache, just absence.
- **blank** — the thinking channel: asked a question, nothing comes.
  Thoughts will not form or assemble; the mind's screen is empty.
- **checked out** — the presence channel: body in the room, attention
  gone. Running on autopilot, conversations passing through without
  landing.
- **mute** — the speech channel: words are unavailable or cost far too
  much. You may know what you would say and still be unable to produce
  it.
- **unreal** — the reality channel: the world at a remove — dreamlike,
  flat-lit, like watching yourself from outside. The dissociative marker;
  worth taking seriously as a depth gauge.

### drained

The depletion axis: capacity is spent, from today's spend or from months
of it. Distinct from sad (nothing is lost, the tank is just empty) and
runs a gradient from ordinary tiredness to burnout — the point where rest
has stopped restoring (`failure-modes/autistic-burnout`,
`telemetry-state/four-autistic-stress-state-signatures`).

**Signs:** everything costs more than it should; standards dropping to
minimum-viable; recovery behaviours (sofa, silence, scrolling) taking
over by default; the question "when did I last feel rested?" having no
recent answer.

- **tired** — ordinary depletion at the end of ordinary effort; a night's
  rest actually fixes it. The baseline word.
- **exhausted** — deep depletion *now*: function visibly degraded, body
  heavy, pushing further producing sharply diminishing returns.
- **weary** — the long-haul form: tired *of* something as much as tired
  from it — a slog that has gone on too long, with a fed-up edge.
- **sleepy** — sleep pressure specifically: heavy eyes, yawning, drifting
  off. You can be sleepy without being depleted — this word is about the
  need for sleep itself.
- **foggy** — cognition dulled by depletion: thinking through cotton
  wool, words and names not arriving, plans hard to hold. *Boundary:*
  tangled-but-active thinking is `confused > muddled`; this is slow and
  clouded.
- **burned out** — the chronic end-state: extended overload has outrun
  recovery, rest no longer restores, and things you could normally do
  (skills, tolerances, sensory margins) have degraded. A season-scale
  word, not a bad-day word.

## sad

Something is lost, missing, or short of what it should be, and the energy
runs low and **inward** — toward tears, heaviness, withdrawal. Sadness is
about an absence; that is what separates it from `overloaded` (too much)
and `fearful` (not yet).

**Boundary:** if there is no loss and no ache — just emptiness or a spent
tank — look at `overloaded > shut down > numb` or `overloaded > drained`.

### down

The general low-mood word: colour and energy drained out of things
without a single nameable loss. The day is greyer, the effort is higher,
the pleasure is quieter.

**Signs:** flat mornings; things you usually enjoy landing dull; more
effort for the same output; a soundtrack of mild negativity.

- **low** — the plainest form: mood a notch or three below baseline,
  noticeable but functional. The honest small word.
- **gloomy** — the low mood has spread onto the world: outlooks,
  forecasts, and futures all render dark.
- **glum** — visibly low: quiet, monosyllabic, uncheerable — the version
  other people can see on your face.
- **heavy** — the physical form: limbs and chest weighted, everything
  requiring lifting. The body word for low mood.
- **teary** — close to crying with or without a clear cause; the water
  table is high and small things breach it.
- **joyless** — going through motions that usually produce pleasure and
  getting nothing back; the reward channel specifically has gone quiet.

### grieving

Sadness with a specific loss at the centre — a person, relationship, era,
ability, or hope. The feeling orbits what is gone and comes in waves
rather than as a steady level.

**Signs:** waves triggered by reminders; the impulse to reach for what is
no longer there; time split into before and after.

- **bereft** — the state of having had something essential removed; the
  hole itself, present tense.
- **raw** — the loss is too fresh to touch: any contact with the subject
  hurts directly, without buffer.
- **heartbroken** — the relational form: a love — romantic or otherwise —
  ended, betrayed, or lost, with the ache centred in the chest.
- **aching** — the persistent dull hurt that runs under function; not a
  wave but the sea level itself.
- **longing** — reaching toward what is absent: missing someone or
  something with an active pull toward it.
- **wistful** — the gentle version: touched by a loss or a might-have-been
  softened by time; sad and almost sweet at once.

### lonely

Sadness about connection: the people side of life is missing, thin, or
not reaching you. Loneliness is about felt connection, not headcount —
it can be sharpest in company.

**Signs:** scrolling contacts without messaging anyone; company leaving
you emptier than solitude; the sense that no one currently *gets* it.

- **isolated** — practically cut off: circumstances (distance, schedule,
  illness) have removed access to your people.
- **excluded** — a specific exclusion: the plan, group, or conversation
  happened and you were not in it.
- **unseen** — present but not registered: contributions and feelings
  passing without acknowledgement; invisible in plain sight.
- **rejected** — the harsher read: not just overlooked but refused — the
  sense that your presence, offer, or self was weighed and turned away.
- **homesick** — displaced from your place and people; the ache points at
  a *where* as much as a who.
- **abandoned** — someone who was supposed to stay went; the exit was
  theirs and the wound carries that betrayal.

### disappointed

Sadness at the gap between expected and actual. Something you hoped for,
counted on, or worked toward came in under the mark — an event, a result,
a person, yourself.

**Signs:** replaying the expected version against the real one; "I
thought this would be different" in some wording; deflation rather than
heat.

- **let down** — a person or promise specifically failed you; the gap has
  a name attached.
- **deflated** — the air went out: anticipation or enthusiasm collapsed
  on contact with the outcome.
- **underwhelmed** — the thing arrived and was less than advertised;
  expectations quietly exceeded reality. (Also the nearest current word
  for *bored* — see the E3 → E4 mapping.)
- **discouraged** — the setback has reached your willingness to continue:
  effort suddenly looks poorly spent.
- **dismayed** — disappointment with alarm in it: the situation is not
  just worse than hoped, it is worse than feared, and you have just seen
  it.
- **downcast** — the visible after-state: head and mood both lowered,
  carrying the setback in posture and tone.

### ashamed

Sadness turned on the self after a fall from standard — yours or the
audience's. The self-conscious cluster: what you did (or are) has been
weighed and found wanting, with the imagined or real gaze of others in
the room.

**Signs:** avoiding eye contact or mention of the event; replaying the
moment with a cringe; urge to hide, undo, or over-apologise.

- **guilty** — about an *act*: you did or failed to do a specific thing
  and it sits on you. Repair urges (confess, fix, apologise) are the
  tell.
- **embarrassed** — the social sting of looking foolish in front of
  others; hot-faced and momentary rather than deep.
- **humiliated** — embarrassment with power in it: made small *by
  someone* in front of others; the injury includes the audience.
- **regretful** — the quieter, longer form of guilt: wishing a past
  choice unmade, with the emphasis on the fork in the road.
- **sheepish** — mild, half-smiling shame at a small caught-out failing;
  the "fair cop" feeling.
- **mortified** — the extreme of embarrassed: wishing the ground would
  open; the memory produces a full-body flinch.

### despairing

The heavy end of the sector: hope itself is failing. The situation — or
everything — reads as beyond repair, and effort feels pointless. Words
from this branch, picked honestly, are a signal worth acting on, not
just recording.

**Signs:** "no point" thoughts generalising across domains; options all
pre-rejected; the future shortening to a grey wall.

- **bleak** — the outlook word: the future renders empty and cold, with
  nothing warm visible on the horizon.
- **worthless** — despair pointed at the self: the ledger reads that you
  contribute nothing and merit nothing. A word to treat as an alarm,
  not a fact.
- **defeated** — the fight is over and you lost it: effort has stopped
  because it stopped working, not because the wish went away.
- **resigned** — the low-energy acceptance of a bad outcome as fixed;
  wanting has been switched off to stop it hurting.
- **anguished** — despair at full volume: acute mental pain that demands
  expression — the wailing register, whether or not any sound comes out.
- **crushed** — flattened by a specific blow: the weight landed and
  something in you gave way under it.

## calm

Regulated, low-arousal, and pleasant: the nervous system is settled and
nothing needs bracing against. Calm is its own core rather than a lack of
feeling — quiet regulated states deserve recording without claiming
happiness (polyvagal "glimmers").

**Boundary:** flatness without the pleasantness — nothing registering,
rather than nothing wrong — is `overloaded > shut down > numb`.

### peaceful

Deep quiet: stillness felt as good. The world and the mind have both
turned their volume down, and there is nowhere you need to be.

**Signs:** long, easy breaths; no pull toward the phone or the next
task; background noise (mental and literal) faded out.

- **serene** — the composed, almost elevated form: clear water all the
  way down, undisturbed by what is passing.
- **still** — the body form: motion and fidget gone, sitting easy in
  one place without effort.
- **unhurried** — time pressure absent: moving through tasks at their
  natural pace with no clock leaning on you.
- **hushed** — the quiet itself as the feeling: early-morning,
  library-quiet, snowfall register.
- **untroubled** — the specific absence of worry: checked the horizon,
  found nothing incoming, believed it.
- **quiet** — the plain word: internal noise low, nothing demanding
  speech or thought; a rest state you would choose.

### content

Enough-ness: what is here is sufficient, and you are not reaching for
more. Warmer than peaceful, less energetic than happy — satisfaction at
rest.

**Signs:** absence of wanting; small sensory pleasures registering
(warmth, food, light); no urge to check what else is on offer.

- **satisfied** — a want was met or a job well finished, and the
  completion still registers; the closed-loop feeling.
- **comfortable** — the body version: physically at ease — warmth,
  softness, no complaint from any quarter.
- **cosy** — comfort plus enclosure: small warm space, blanket register,
  the outside kept pleasantly out.
- **fulfilled** — the deep version: life or work currently meaning
  something; the satisfaction of purpose, not just appetite.
- **mellow** — softened and easy: edges rounded off, tolerance high,
  nothing worth hurrying or arguing about.
- **at home** — belonging where you are — place or people fitting so well
  that no adjustment or performance is needed.

### relaxed

Tension leaving or gone: the release state. Where peaceful is depth of
quiet, relaxed is the *unwinding* — shoulders dropping, jaw loosening,
the system standing down.

**Signs:** noticing tension only as it releases; slower speech and
movement; sighs of the good kind.

- **at ease** — no guard up: comfortable in the situation and with the
  people in it, nothing being monitored.
- **loose** — the muscular form: body unclenched, movement free, the
  physical opposite of braced.
- **unwound** — the after-state of stress released: the coil let out,
  usually at the end of something demanding.
- **soothed** — settled *by* something — a person, bath, music, tea; the
  comfort has an identifiable source.
- **refreshed** — restored and clean-feeling after rest or a break;
  morning-after-good-sleep register.
- **recharged** — capacity visibly back: energy and willingness restored
  and available for spending again.

### safe

Protection felt from the inside: no threat in view, guards stood down,
and support that would catch you if something came. The polyvagal ground
state — the felt condition under which everything else can settle.

**Signs:** startle threshold high; breath low and slow; willing to close
your eyes, turn your back, or let attention soften.

- **settled** — landed after turbulence: change or upheaval has finished
  moving and you have re-established ground.
- **protected** — something or someone stands between you and harm, and
  you can feel it standing there.
- **sheltered** — out of the weather — literal or situational; the storm
  may exist, but not where you are.
- **held** — supported by a person or people: carried emotionally,
  contained, not managing alone.
- **grounded** — connected downward into your own body and the present;
  stable footing, low centre of gravity, here.
- **steady** — even and reliable from the inside: no wobble, able to be
  leaned on — by yourself or others.

### focused

Chosen, regulated engagement: attention gathered onto one thing *by
consent*, with the rest of the world politely queued. Effortful or
effortless, it remains yours — you can put it down when something else
matters, and it tends to leave you fed rather than spent.

**Signs:** single-tasking without strain; time passing quickly but
check-outable; interruptions annoying but survivable; ending the session
by choice.

**Boundary:** if you *cannot* put it down — meals slide, switching feels
physically resistant — that is `overloaded > stuck > hyperfocus`.

- **absorbed** — deeply immersed in the task by choice; the world quiets
  around the work and the immersion feels good.
- **engaged** — actively involved with traction: the task has your
  interest and effort, and is giving progress back.
- **in flow** — the effortless channel: skill and challenge matched,
  self-consciousness gone, the work almost doing itself.
- **clear** — the fog's opposite: thinking crisp, priorities obvious,
  decisions available without strain.
- **sharp** — cognitively quick: fast recall, good edges on ideas,
  wit available; the high-performance end of clear.
- **present** — attention resting in the actual moment — sensations,
  people, surroundings — rather than in past, future, or phone.

### curious

Appetite for finding out: attention leaning toward something because it
is interesting, with openness rather than need. The exploratory pleasant
state — low threat, forward-tilted.

**Signs:** questions arriving unbidden; following links or threads for
their own sake; "I wonder—" as the prevailing sentence shape.

- **keen** — eager to get into it: appetite for the task or topic sharp
  and ready.
- **intrigued** — hooked by something specific that does not yet add up;
  a puzzle has your attention and is holding it pleasantly.
- **open** — receptive without an agenda: willing to be shown, changed,
  or surprised; opinions held loosely.
- **wondering** — the reflective drift: turning a question over slowly
  for the pleasure of it, no answer urgently required.
- **fascinated** — fully captured *with enjoyment*: the subject has all
  your attention and you would happily give it more.
- **drawn in** — the pull itself: something is reeling your attention
  closer and you are letting it.

## happy

Positive affect with energy in it: pleasure, connection, achievement,
anticipation. Where calm is the pleasant *low*-arousal core, happy is the
pleasant *charged* one — things are actively good.

### hopeful

Positive feeling pointed at the future: the road ahead looks like it
rises. The good thing has not happened yet — that is what distinguishes
this branch from proud or excited.

**Signs:** plans feel worth making; setbacks read as temporary; the
future tense showing up in your sentences with light in it.

- **optimistic** — the general setting: expecting things to work out,
  defaults tilted positive.
- **encouraged** — hope fed by evidence: something specific went right
  and the trend line looks better because of it.
- **inspired** — ignited by an idea, person, or work: the urge to make or
  do something has just been lit.
- **uplifted** — raised by something outside you — music, kindness, a
  view; carried upward for a while.
- **buoyant** — hope in the body: light, floating, hard to sink;
  resilience with a spring in it.
- **heartened** — reassured at the heart level: something restored your
  faith in a person, plan, or humanity generally.

### excited

Charged anticipation or delight: energy up, system sparkling, something
good happening or coming. The high-arousal pleasant state — enjoyable,
and also worth tracking because its body-feel overlaps with anxiety's.

**Signs:** talking faster and more; trouble sitting still for good
reasons; counting down *toward* something rather than away from it.

- **eager** — ready and impatient to start; the want-to leaning forward
  against the not-yet.
- **thrilled** — delight at high intensity about something specific that
  is happening or confirmed.
- **exhilarated** — the rush form: aliveness flooding in from speed,
  achievement, cold water, big news.
- **energetic** — energised toward action: motivation and body both up,
  ready to charge at the thing.
- **giddy** — excitement fizzing over into lightheadedness and laughter;
  slightly silly with it.
- **joyful** — the peak: joy at altitude, walking on air, grinning
  without meaning to.

### playful

Lightness and fun: the register where jokes, games, and silliness live.
Low stakes, high warmth — the state in which you tease, riff, and
delight in nonsense.

**Signs:** joking arriving naturally; willingness to be daft; play for
its own sake looking like a good use of time.

- **amused** — something has genuinely tickled you; smiling or laughing
  at it, inside or out.
- **silly** — happily unserious: doing or saying daft things for the joy
  of it, dignity temporarily off duty.
- **mischievous** — playfulness with a scheme in it: minor trouble,
  pranks, or wind-ups under friendly intent.
- **lighthearted** — the ambient form: cares weightless, mood easy,
  nothing being taken too seriously.
- **tickled** — disproportionately delighted by something small and
  specific; charmed into giggles by a detail.
- **cheeky** — playful impertinence: pushing the line with a grin,
  banter with a bit of nerve in it.

### grateful

Warm appreciation for what has been given or simply is: attention resting
on the good received rather than the good pursued. Includes the release
register — relief — where the good thing is a bad thing not happening.

**Signs:** spontaneous thank-yous (said or felt); noticing what is
working; the impulse to tell someone what they mean to you.

- **thankful** — plain gratitude with an object: someone or something
  did you good and you feel it.
- **blessed** — gratitude at the scale of life: aware of holding more
  good fortune than you can claim credit for.
- **touched** — moved by someone's care or gesture: the small sting
  behind the eyes when kindness lands.
- **relieved** — the weight lifted: the feared thing resolved, the wait
  ended, the news good; exhale as a feeling.
- **lucky** — aware that chance broke your way; gratitude with dice in
  it.
- **humbled** — gratitude that resizes you: generosity or grace received
  beyond what you feel you earned.

### proud

Positive feeling about your own worth, work, or people: achievement
registered and owned. The confidence-and-accomplishment branch (the old
Willcox "powerful" sector lives here).

**Signs:** standing straighter; wanting to show someone; self-talk
crediting rather than discounting the win.

- **successful** — a real thing got finished or achieved, and the
  completion is felt as fact, not fluke.
- **confident** — forward-looking self-trust: believing your abilities
  will be sufficient for what is coming.
- **capable** — the quieter, in-motion form: handling things and noticing
  that you are handling them.
- **triumphant** — victory with adrenaline still on it: a contest,
  challenge, or struggle just won.
- **chuffed** — the British register: quietly delighted with yourself or
  your luck, pleased as punch and slightly modest about it.
- **courageous** — courage in the pleasant key: willing to take the risk,
  say the thing, make the move — and enjoying that willingness.

### loving

Warmth flowing toward others: affection, tenderness, and attachment in
their felt form. Pointed at partners, children, friends, animals — anyone
the heart has decided belongs to it.

**Signs:** urge to touch, message, or do something for them; softening
when they come to mind; their wins landing as your good news.

- **affectionate** — warmth seeking expression: hugs, endearments, small
  gestures queuing up to be delivered.
- **tender** — gentle care toward something precious and slightly
  breakable: the soft-handed register of love.
- **warm** — the ambient form: general goodwill and openness toward the
  people around you; heart-temperature raised a degree.
- **adoring** — devotion looking upward: delighting in someone with a
  touch of awe, faults currently invisible.
- **smitten** — captivated and lit up by someone, usually early and
  usually obvious; the falling register.
- **doting** — love in caretaking form: fond attention lavished on
  someone, pleasure taken in their smallest doings.

## Deliberate near-pairs

Cross-sector pairs that are close on purpose, with the test that
separates them. If a pair here still feels indistinguishable in use,
that is catalogue feedback worth filing.

| This… | …not that | Test |
|-------|-----------|------|
| `boiling over > tetchy` | `irritated > snappy` | Is there a grievance you could state? Snappy has one; tetchy is capacity gone. |
| `boiling over > on edge` | `anxious > keyed up` | Braced because of accumulated load (on edge) vs anticipation of a specific future thing (keyed up). |
| `boiling over > wound up` | `anxious > tense` | Wound up is charge that wants discharge; tense is a brace against something feared. |
| `overstimmed > twitchy` | `anxious > jittery` | Does it track the sensory environment (twitchy) or your thoughts (jittery)? Remove the input and see which one fades. |
| `overstimmed` (any) | `anxious` (any) | Distress rising/falling with present input = overstimmed; persisting regardless, fed by what-ifs = anxious (`failure-modes/anxiety-vs-sensory-overload`). |
| `boiling over > meltdown` | `panicked > frantic` | Frantic is goal-directed rushing at a threat; meltdown is involuntary discharge with reasoning offline. |
| `stuck > hyperfocus` | `focused > absorbed` / `in flow` | Can you put it down when something else needs you, and does it leave you fed or wrecked? Choice and cost. |
| `stuck > ruminating` | `curious > wondering` | Wondering is open and pleasant; ruminating replays distress without the facts changing. |
| `shut down > frozen` | `helpless > trapped` | Trapped has closed exits in the situation; frozen is the action system itself offline. |
| `shut down > numb` | `down > joyless` | Joyless still aches — pleasure specifically is missing; numb registers nothing at all, ache included. |
| `drained > foggy` | `confused > muddled` | Muddled is tangled-but-active thinking; foggy is slow, clouded, and quiet. |
| `drained > weary` | `frustrated > fed up` | Both are "too long of this"; fed up has anger's energy in it, weary has none left. |
| `drained > burned out` | `sad > despairing` | Burnout is capacity loss (rest stopped working); despair is hope loss. They can co-occur — record both. |
| `overwhelmed > literal` | `confused > muddled` | Literal still parses fine but flat — nuance is gone; muddled cannot get the pieces to line up at all. |
